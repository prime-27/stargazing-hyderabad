    Within a drive

    Where the stars come back

    The further you get from the city's glow, the more you'll see. Every one of these is a big step up from a Hyderabad terrace.

    ≈ 80 km~2 h drive · Vikarabad

    Ananthagiri Hills

    The closest real escape. Forested hills west of the city; you'll still see Hyderabad's glow on the eastern horizon, but the sky overhead is far darker than at home.

    Good for a first trip or a weeknight. Resorts nearby fill up on weekends.

    Directions
    ≈ 115 km~2.5 h drive · Medak

    Pocharam Wildlife Sanctuary

    A lake and forest north of Medak, far enough from the city that the sky gets noticeably darker. Open water gives you clear, low horizons.

    Check sanctuary visiting hours and where you're allowed to stay after dark.

    Directions
    ≈ 150 km~3 h drive · Nalgonda

    Nagarjuna Sagar

    Wide skies over one of India's largest reservoirs. Big horizons are ideal for watching the Moon or planets rise and for meteor showers.

    Stay overnight in town and drive a few minutes away from the lights.

    Directions
    ≈ 210 km~4.5 h drive · Nallamala hills

    Srisailam & the Nallamala forest

    Among the darkest skies you can reach from Hyderabad, deep in the Nallamala hills. On a clear, moonless night the Milky Way is easy to see.

    The forest road through Amrabad Tiger Reserve closes at night. Check the gate timings and plan to stay over.

    Directions
    ≈ 215 km~4.5 h drive · Mulugu

    Lakhnavaram Lake

    A forest lake with island cottages, well beyond Warangal. Make a weekend of it: stargaze from the shore and sleep a few steps away.

    Book cottages ahead, especially from November to February.

    In the cityNaubat Pahad

    B.M. Birla Planetarium

    Not a dark sky, but a good place to learn the constellations before your first trip out. From a terrace you can still see the Moon, planets and the brightest stars.

    Sky calendar

    Meteor showers worth staying up for

    Peak nights for the year's main showers, with how much moonlight to expect on each one. The rate is what you'd see under a perfectly dark sky. Away from the city, expect fewer.

      October – February

      Best season

      Dry, clear and cool. Orion, Taurus and the Pleiades rule the evening, and the Geminids arrive in December. Bring a jacket to the hills.

      March – May

      Hazy evenings

      The heat brings haze, but the centre of the Milky Way rises before dawn from March. Scorpius follows after midnight.

      June – September

      Monsoon

      Cloudy most nights. Watch the forecast for breaks; when it clears, Scorpius and Sagittarius sit high in the evening sky.

      Your first night out

      1. Pick a moonless night

        Check the moon-free window above. A bright Moon washes out everything faint, so aim for the week around a new moon.

      2. Check the clouds, not just rain

        A dry forecast can still be cloudy. Look at cloud cover by the hour before you leave.

      3. Let your eyes adjust

        Give it 20–30 minutes away from screens and headlights. Use only red light. Tap Red light at the top of this page to turn it red.

      4. Binoculars before a telescope

        A pair of 7×50 or 10×50 binoculars shows star clusters, the Moon's craters and Jupiter's moons. That's often more than a cheap telescope will.

      5. Learn a few signposts

        Start with Orion in winter and Scorpius in summer, then find your way around with a free star map like Stellarium.

      6. Go prepared

        Go with company. Take water, warm layers and something to lie on, respect forest and private land, and leave nothing behind.
